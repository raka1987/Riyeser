Help

Step-by-step help for the Riyeser app. In the app, tap ? for help on the screen you're on. Still stuck? Email info@riyeser.com.

Getting started

  1. Sign in, then tap New project and give it a name, site and client.
  2. Tap Upload drawings and choose one or more PDFs or images at once. Tap each page you want to use so it has a green tick, then tap Add. Each one becomes a level.
  3. Tap Set scale, tap two points a known distance apart, and type the distance. Lengths are then measured for you.
  4. New here? Turn on Getting started guide in Settings, or try the demo first.

Choosing which drawings are in use

Tap Drawings next to the level list to see every drawing in the project. Drawings with a green tick are in use. Untick one to put it aside, for example an old revision: its markup is kept and comes back when you tick it again.

Adding items

  1. Tap Add item, choose the trade, then the item, for example Data outlet or Distribution board.
  2. Tap the drawing where it goes. The new item pulses so you can see it.
  3. Fill in the details: location, height, specification. Tap Save when you're done.

Use Add another like this to place a row of the same item quickly. Tap Key on the plan to see what each symbol means.

Notes on the drawing

Tap Note, then tap or drag where it goes. Pick a text size from the chart (6 to 40), and choose a white background so the words stand out, or a see-through colour. Adjust Background strength to suit. Tick Pointer arrow to an area to point at something, then drag the arrow tip to the spot.

To move a note, tap it, then Move or resize. Drag it as many times as you like, drag the corner dot to make it wider or narrower, then tap Done. The box shrinks to fit the words.

Markup tools

Tap Markup on the toolbar for:

  • Cloud: tap round an area, then Finish, to add a revision cloud with a label.
  • Arrow: tap the start, then where it points, and add a label.
  • Dimension: tap two points. The measured length is printed on the drawing.
  • Pen and Highlighter: draw freehand with your finger, pen or mouse.
  • Stamp: Approved, Rejected, As built, Not in scope, Hold and more, with the date and your initials.
  • Count: tap each thing to count it, such as downlights, with a running total. Tap a tick again to remove it.
  • Area m²: tap round a room to get its floor area.
  • Measure: measure along a route without saving it.

Pick a colour and size before you draw. Tap any markup afterwards to change its label, colour or size, or to delete it. Markups print on the as-built and shop drawings with your notes.

Straight lines keeps runs and markups level, upright or at 45°. Hold Shift on a keyboard, or tick it in the panel. Snap joins points to nearby items and run corners.

Repeat items in a row

Open an item and tap Repeat in a row. Choose how many copies, the spacing in metres and the direction. Each copy gets the next label.

Layers and comparing revisions

In Showing everything, use Layers to hide notes, markups, counts or stage areas. Under Compare with another revision, pick an earlier drawing to lay over the current one: blue is only on this drawing, red is only on the other one, and dark is on both.

Cables, pipes and ducts

  1. Tap Draw a run, choose what it is, then tap along the route on the drawing.
  2. Start and finish on items such as a rack, pit or outlet, so the run is connected at both ends.
  3. For a run that changes level, finish it at a riser and continue it on the next level.

Cables inside a conduit or tray take its depth or height automatically.

Ports, outlets and racks

Set up a rack or board

Open the rack, cabinet or board and add what it has: patch panels and fibre trays for comms, ways or circuits for a switchboard, loops or zones for a fire panel.

Connect an outlet to its port

  1. Open the outlet and choose how many ports it has.
  2. For each port, tap Choose rack port, then pick the rack, the panel and the port number. Grey ports are already in use.
  3. Check the connectors at each end and add the test result, then tap Connect.

A second cable from the same outlet to the same place follows the same route automatically. From the rack, Port map shows every port and what it goes to.

Inspections, tests and photos

  1. Open an item and tap Inspect. Work through the checklist and mark each check Pass, Fail or N/A.
  2. Record any test readings and add photos, especially before a wall, ceiling or trench is closed.
  3. Hold points stop the item from being covered until they're signed off.

The To do button shows everything still outstanding on the project.

RFIs and defects

  1. Open the RFIs or Defects list and tap New, or raise one from an item.
  2. Describe the question or problem, link the items, add photos and a due date.
  3. Use Send to client to share a link the client can answer without an account.

Tap any RFI, defect or issue and the item pulses on the drawing.

Trace route and schematics

Trace route on a cable shows that one cable from start to end and flashes it on the plan. On a rack or multi-port outlet, pick which connection to trace.

The Diagram tab shows the schematic. Use Show to see everything, one item, one area or one level, and choose the service.

Reports and handover

From the Project menu you can print or save as PDF:

  • the handover pack, with plans, registers, schedules, schematics, QA reports and photos;
  • as-built drawings;
  • port and circuit schedules and labels;
  • RFI, defect and test reports.

To download the current drawings with your notes, open Project, then As-built drawings (Shop drawings in a quote). Set Notes to Include notes, then tap Print or save as PDF. Drawings that only have notes on them are included too.

Before you export, the handover pack lists anything still outstanding. Tap one to fix it, then use Back to export review.

Quotes and shop drawings

  1. Tap New project and set Type to Quote. It shows a Quote tag on your project list.
  2. Upload the drawings and mark up the proposed items and runs as usual.
  3. From the Project menu, print Shop drawings (stamped "For quotation only. Not for construction.") and the Quantities schedule, which counts every item and measures every run by level. Export it as CSV to price it in your own spreadsheet.
  4. Won the work? Tap Convert to live job in the Project menu. Everything you marked up carries straight over.

Stages and areas

For staged buildings, tap Area on the toolbar under the drawing (or Project, then Stages and areas). Tap each corner of the area, then Finish and save. Name it (for example Stage 1), pick a colour or tap the rainbow circle for any colour, and choose how strong the fill shade is, from outline only to solid. Choose Hatched for Not in scope, By others or Future works. Use the same name on other drawings to group them. To change or delete an area, open Project, then Stages and areas.

Items inside an area count to it, and runs that cross areas are split by length. The quantities schedule then has a column for each stage, the shop drawings show the coloured areas with a key, and Showing everything lets you view one stage at a time.

Working offline

Everything you record is saved on your device straight away. With no signal, keep working; it syncs when you're back in range. Use Save & exit before leaving to make sure everything has synced.

Settings

Tap the gear to switch Simple mode, Sunlight mode, hints, the guide, plan symbols, AS 5488 service colours, the drawing reader and units.

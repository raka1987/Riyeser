Riyeser Estimate help

Measure jobs on a plan, a photo or the map, and turn them into quotes. Version {{ riyeser_version }}.

An estimate in six steps

  1. Customer: pick the customer (or type a new one) and the job address.
  2. How to measure: on the map, on a plan or photo, type in sizes, or import a scan from a room-scanning app. You can switch any time.
  3. Measure: everything saves as you go. The totals panel (at the bottom on a phone) adds up the whole job. In the trade this is called a takeoff.
  4. Template: pick a trade (roofing, fencing, painting, tiling and more). Templates you used lately come first.
  5. Price: lines grouped into labour, materials and other. Change a rate, the waste or add a markup; your profit and margin show here for you only.
  6. Send: look at the PDF, email or print it, or mark it as sent. When the customer says yes, open the quote and press Mark accepted; make the invoice in Riyeser Invoices or Accounting, or a job in Riyeser Bookings (they open the same company file).

Measured again? Press Update quote on step 5: the lines Estimate made are replaced; lines you typed into the quote yourself stay. An accepted quote is never changed: Duplicate the estimate to quote again.

Typing in sizes

  • Room: length, width and height give the floor, ceiling, walls (less the doors and windows you list), perimeter and skirting (less door widths).
  • Area, circle, triangle: add a depth for a volume (concrete, soil, mulch). Also count it as makes an area count as floor or wall area, so room-based templates use it.
  • Length: add a height for a face area, or a spacing to count posts or joists. Count and Hours are for points, fixtures and labour.
  • Roof face: plan area (from above) and pitch, in degrees or rise over run (6/12). Fence: length, post spacing and gates.
  • Plain numbers are in your units (metres, or feet for imperial). You can type units: 2400mm, 3.6 m, 12' 6", 10 1/2".
  • Laser measurers: one that works as a Bluetooth keyboard types straight into the box; Enter moves to the next box. (Direct Bluetooth links and phone AR come later.)

Plans and photos

  • Open a plan or photo: a PDF (every page) or a picture. It stays in this company file.
  • Set the scale before drawing: press Set scale, tap both ends of something you know (a dimension line, a door) and type its real length. For a PDF drawn to scale and printed at full size, you can pick the drawing scale instead (1:100). Check one known size after.
  • Tools: Area (tap the corners; double-tap or Finish to close), Cut-out (cuts a hole from the last area), Length, Count, Roof face, Fence. Undo/Redo work on points and on saved measurements (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y). Name and colour each one, and use a group to keep sets apart (e.g. power and light points).
  • On the quote's measurements page, the plan is shown with your lines on it.

The map

  • Search the address, then draw with the same tools. The map needs the internet; everything else works offline.
  • Roof face: draw each face, type its pitch (a picture from above can't show it), then say what each edge is: ridge, hip, valley, eave (gutter), rake (barge) or wall flashing. True area = plan area ÷ cos(pitch); rakes are lengthened by the same factor, hips and valleys by √(1 + tan²(pitch) ÷ 2). A hip drawn on two faces is counted once.
  • Fence: posts = each straight run ÷ post spacing, rounded up, plus one, plus a post for each gate (a rule of thumb: change it with extra corner posts).
  • Map sizes are estimates from satellite imagery: check them on site. The imagery may be years old, trees and eaves hide edges, and buildings can lean in the picture. The quote says so too.
  • Imagery: Esri World Imagery (attribution on the map). Only your own lines are saved, never the imagery; the quote's measurements page draws your lines on a plain background.

Importing a scan

A room scan from your phone or tablet becomes rooms in the estimate: walls with their lengths and heights, doors and windows with their sizes, floor and ceiling areas and the perimeter for skirting. Check them, fix anything that's wrong, then press Use these rooms. Painting, flooring, tiling, plastering and skirting templates then price them like rooms you typed in. You also get a 3D mock-up to show the customer (pick a wall and floor colour) and a floor plan, which goes on the quote's measurements page.

The file is read on this computer and kept with the estimate (up to 100 MB). Nothing is sent anywhere.

What each kind of file gives

  • Full rooms (walls, doors and windows with sizes): a USDZ from an iPhone or iPad app that uses Apple's RoomPlan, and RoomPlan JSON files.
  • Rooms from a 2D plan: a DXF floor plan (Polycam, Magicplan, CAD). A plan has no heights, so walls get a typical ceiling height and doors and windows typical heights: check them.
  • A room list: a CSV with room names and areas (Magicplan's statistics export). The rooms' shapes aren't known, so the floor plan shows a rectangle of the same size.
  • Estimated from a 3D mesh: GLB, glTF, OBJ, DAE, STL or a mesh USDZ (Polycam, Canvas, 3D Scanner and similar apps). Riyeser finds the floor, ceiling and walls by looking for large flat surfaces. Sizes are marked estimated from a 3D mesh, check on site, and doors and windows aren't found: add them by hand. Furniture against walls and gaps in the scan can throw the sizes out.
  • PDF plans open in On a plan or photo: set the scale from one known size and draw.
  • Can't be read: DWG (save it as DXF), FBX (export GLB or OBJ), point clouds (PLY, LAS, E57, XYZ), IFC, SketchUp and Revit files. Compressed GLB files (Draco) can't be read either: export without compression.

How to export from your app

  • iPhone or iPad apps that use RoomPlan (they draw the room as clean boxes while you scan; they need a LiDAR iPhone Pro or iPad Pro): when the scan is done, choose Export or Share and pick USDZ. If the app offers a choice, pick the plain model (sometimes called parametric or room model), not the textured mesh. Save it to Files, AirDrop it to your computer, or open Riyeser on the phone and choose the file from Files.
  • Polycam: open the capture, tap Export, and choose GLB (or OBJ). For a captured room on a LiDAR device, the floor plan export gives a DXF (a paid Polycam plan), which comes in as rooms from a plan. Keep the mesh at medium detail or less for files under 100 MB.
  • Magicplan: open the project and choose Export. Pick DXF for the floor plan, CSV for the room statistics (areas and perimeters), or USDZ or OBJ for the 3D model. The DXF has no dimensions written on it; Riyeser measures the lines.
  • Canvas (now called Twindo): export the scan as OBJ; it comes in as an estimate from a mesh. Drawings from its paid Scan to CAD service come as DWG or PDF: save the DWG as DXF first, or open the PDF on a plan.
  • Any CAD app: save the floor plan as an ASCII DXF. Put room names inside the rooms, and doors and windows on layers named Doors and Windows, so they're found.

No scanner handy? Download a sample scan (a bedroom with a door and a window) and import it.

Templates and prices

  • Each line: quantity = measured amount in the line's unit × factor × (1 + waste %), rounded up to whole packs when there's a pack size, then × your rate. A line's minimum charge lifts a small line; the template's minimum charge tops up the whole quote.
  • Riyeser's starting templates have no prices (every business is different). Their waste %, coverage and pack sizes are rules of thumb: check them.
  • Your cost on a line gives the profit and margin on step 5 (never on the quote). Import a supplier price list (CSV) updates costs and rates in one go. Save these prices as a template on step 5 copies a past job's prices for next time.
